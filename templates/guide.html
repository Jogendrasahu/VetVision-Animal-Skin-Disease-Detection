Rule of 5

The 5 Golden Rules of Veterinary Photography

Convolutional neural networks rely on micro-textures of epidermal crusting, erythema, and hair follicular patterns. High-quality input directly yields high-confidence triage.

Rule 1 • Diffuse Lighting

Bright, Even Lighting

Shoot in indirect daylight or bright indoor room lighting. Avoid harsh phone camera flash, which reflects off shiny sebum and washes out skin redness (erythema).

Rule 2 • Part Fur & Expose Lesions

Direct Skin Contact

Gently part thick outer coat using your fingers or a clean comb to directly expose the epidermal margin, scales, papules, pustules, or ulcers to the camera lens.

Rule 3 • Macro Distance & Focus

10–20 cm Distance

Position camera 10 to 20 cm from the lesion. Tap the smartphone screen directly on the lesion center to lock focus and prevent background blur.

Rule 4 • Multi-Angle Coverage

Capture 2–5 Perspectives

Use our Multi-Image mode! Capture one 90° perpendicular overhead shot, two 45° oblique margin shots, and one wider contextual body reference.

✅ Recommended Practices
  • Calm animal gently before snapping to eliminate motion tremor.
  • Clean camera lens with a microfiber cloth before capture.
  • Ensure eyes are open naturally when screening ocular conditions.
  • Capture healthy adjacent skin within frame for color comparison.
❌ Avoid These Mistakes
  • Do not upload zoomed-in screenshots from digital messaging apps.
  • Avoid heavy digital zoom which pixelates fine epidermal scales.
  • Avoid shots where dark room shadows obscure the animal's coat.
  • Do not upload images with human hands completely covering the lesion.
Triage Assessment

Clinical Triage Urgency Matrix

Understand when a skin or eye finding warrants scheduled routine care versus an immediate trip to the 24/7 veterinary emergency room.

Tier 1: Routine Care Non-Urgent

Mild dry flaking, localized chin acne comedones, minimal dandruff without hair loss or intense itching.

Action: Monitor and discuss at your next scheduled wellness visit.
Tier 2: Prompt Veterinary Visit Within 24–48 Hours

Expanding circular alopecia (ringworm), intense pruritus causing self-trauma, foul odor from ears, hot spots, yellow ocular discharge.

Action: Schedule an appointment with your veterinarian within 1 to 2 days.
Tier 3: Emergency Hospital Immediate (Today)

Rapidly swelling face/throat (anaphylaxis), cloudy or ulcerated cornea with blepharospasm, lethargy, fever >39.5°C, extensive oozing pyoderma.

Action: Transport immediately to nearest 24/7 veterinary trauma center.
Model Explainability

Interpreting Model Confidence Scores

How to understand softmax probabilities, differential rankings, and consensus indicators.

🟢

> 75% Confidence

Strong convolutional feature alignment. The visual hallmarks match historical training cases closely. Still requires veterinary cytology confirmation.

🟡

50% – 75% Confidence

Moderate certainty. Lesion shares visual overlap with multiple differentials (e.g. bacterial dermatitis vs fungal infection). Review Top-3 differentials closely.

🔴

< 50% Confidence

Ambiguous / Inconclusive. Low confidence indicates atypical lesion presentation, sub-optimal lighting, or heavy fur obstruction. Retake photo following our 5 Golden Rules.
